1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

   The key breakthrough was deciding what to cut during the planning stage. At the beginning, I explained my full idea to Claude in Plan Mode, and it told me that the original scope would probably take two or three weeks to build. I realised that I needed to simplify it.

   Because I do not have a strong drawing or art background, I chose a black-and-white Chinese ink style. It was visually distinctive, but still simple enough for the website to render without relying heavily on AI-generated images. Once I decided on this direction, the rest of the development became much smoother.

   This turned out to be an important scope decision. Even with this simpler approach, I still used almost all of my available tokens. If I had chosen a more complicated visual or interaction style, I probably would not have been able to finish the prototype.

2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

   I want to become a software developer who can manage AI resources, especially tokens, more carefully.

   In this project, a large amount of my token budget was used during the early planning stage. I used the Opus model for extensive planning, and one planning task ran for around 16 minutes and used about $20 worth of tokens. A lot of that work involved reading files that were not actually necessary for the task.

   As the person directing the agent, I should have noticed this earlier, interrupted the process, and given it a smaller and more focused task. Using a stronger model for longer does not automatically produce a better result. This project taught me that directing an agent also means managing its time, context and token budget. If I manage those resources better, I can leave more room for implementation, testing and final improvements.
